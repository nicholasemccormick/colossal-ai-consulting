§ 02 — What changed

Building software stopped requiring a developer.

Two years ago this advice would have been irresponsible. You would have needed to learn to code, or hire someone at $80–150 an hour who did not understand your business and left halfway through.

That is no longer the constraint. You describe what you need in plain English and the AI writes it, runs it, and fixes it when it breaks. The skill that matters now is knowing what to build and how to describe it precisely — which is a business skill, not an engineering one. You already have it.

What you do not have is someone who has done it fifty times sitting next to you the first time you try. That is the entire product here.

§ 03 — The four weeks

You ship in week one. Not week four.

Every week is a build week. Nothing is theory, and you work on your own business the whole way through.

Week 01

Audit and first build

Total your stack and pick the one tool worth replacing first — usually not the most expensive one, but the one that fits your business worst. Get set up, and ship something small that works by Friday.

Week 02

The replacement

Build the real thing. Your data, your workflow, your language — not a generic template with your logo on it. This is where people realise how much of their current tool they were working around.

Week 03

Make it real

Get it online, get your team into it, handle logins and backups. The difference between a demo and something your business actually runs on is week three.

Week 04

Own it

Learn to change it without me — fix it when it breaks, extend it when the business changes. You leave with the working tool and the ability to build the next one.

§ 05 — The rhythm

Two group calls a week. One-to-one when you’re stuck.

Ten people building at the same time means the question you feel stupid asking gets asked by someone else on Thursday, and everybody learns from the answer.

Monday — Build session

Full group. The week’s method demonstrated live on a real build, then your assignment.

Midweek — 1:1

Thirty minutes with me on your specific build. Book it the weeks you need it.

Thursday — Debrief

Full group. What broke and how it got fixed. Most weeks everyone hits the same wall within a day of each other.

§ 06 — Straight answers

What this does and does not do.

  • You will still pay for some things. An AI subscription, hosting, a domain. Call it $20–60 a month total. The point is replacing hundreds with tens — not reaching zero, which nobody does.
  • You will own what you build. The code, the data, and the ability to change it. No seat pricing, no annual increase, no migration if you want to leave.
  • Not everything is worth replacing. Accounting and payroll are cheap, regulated, and someone else’s liability. I will tell you when to keep paying — usually in week one.
  • Nothing is held back. You get the full method. The point is that you do not need me in month five.
§ 07 — Fit

This is not for everyone.

Ten seats means I would rather leave one empty than fill it badly.

A good fit if…

  • You own or run the business and can decide without a committee
  • You can commit roughly 3–5 hours a week for four weeks
  • You have at least one tool you resent paying for
  • You are willing to be bad at something for about a week

Not a fit if…

  • You want it built for you — that is a different engagement, and I am happy to talk about it
  • You need it to replace regulated software like payroll or accounting
  • You cannot make the two group calls most weeks
  • You are looking for a course to watch rather than a thing to build
